    A chronicle of Britain, 2027 to 2035

    The Great Whiplash

    How AI agents, humanoid robots and a government playing catch-up remade the British economy in nine years.

    Speculative fiction. Every headline, quote and figure on this page is invented.

    Before the flood

    In 2026, AI companies earned about $100 billion. That sounded like a lot, but it was a rounding error in a world economy worth over $100 trillion. The number that mattered was the growth rate. The leading AI companies were growing several times over every year, and the fastest, Anthropic, was selling 14 times more than a year earlier. This story assumes the whole industry now grows tenfold a year, and that nothing stops it.

    On a chart like this, growth looks steady: each bar is a similar step up. In real money, each step is enormous. Going from $100 billion to $1 trillion adds more revenue in one year than the industry had earned in its whole history, and the year after that adds ten times as much again.

    Picture a football stadium being filled with water, where the water doubles every minute and the stadium is full after 50 minutes. Five minutes before it is full, the stadium is only about 3% full. Someone standing in the stands would see a few puddles on the pitch and think there was plenty of time.

    This chronicle imagines that 2026 was minute 45. AI agents take over routine office work, Chinese factories turn out humanoid robots by the million, and Britain, a country with plenty of AI users and almost no AI owners, has to work out how to keep its economy standing.

    Where the money comes from

    That revenue does not appear from nowhere. Most of it is paid by managers who buy AI agents to do work that people used to do: answering customers, processing claims, drafting contracts, checking accounts. When a firm spends £10,000 a year on agents instead of £40,000 on a salary, the AI company's revenue goes up and a job goes away.

    So the two numbers move together. The bigger the flow of money to AI companies, the more wages it has replaced, and the more people are out of work. In this story, every tenfold jump in AI revenue is matched by another wave of redundancies, starting with the best-paid office jobs and spreading outwards to the shops, cafés and landlords those workers used to pay.

    The chart beside the text moves as you read. It tracks AI revenue, computing power, the number of AI agents at work, humanoid robots in service, and the stock market value of AI and robotics firms. It also tracks the size of the largest AI model, which grows from about 10 trillion parameters in 2026 to a quadrillion in 2034, ten times the number of synapses in a human brain, before levelling off. Even as the giants grow, more of the everyday work moves to smaller copies running on computers that ordinary businesses and households own.

    2027

    The Boom That Feels Wrong

    AI company revenue goes from about $100 billion in 2026 to $1 trillion in 2027. Almost all of it comes from the same buyer: a manager with a budget and a team doing routine office work. Agents now draft the contracts, reconcile the accounts, answer the customers and write the reports. They do it for a fraction of a salary, and they do not need a desk.

    In Britain the effect is strange. Share prices climb and company profits hit records, but hardly anyone is hiring. Graduate schemes are quietly cancelled. People who still have jobs keep them, but they stop expecting pay rises. The country uses AI heavily but owns very little of it, so most of the money flows to firms in California.

    It is also the year the risks stop being theoretical. Insiders at the AI labs had warned of a major AI-enabled incident within a year, most likely an attack on banking, power or water. It arrives in June. Meanwhile the data centres being built to meet demand are straining the grid and the water supply, and the people living next to them are the first to notice.

    Feb 2027London

    Corporate profit margins hit record 22% as firms cut staff costs by a third

    Results season shows the same pattern from insurers to housebuilders: headcount down, output up, margins at levels never recorded before. Finance directors describe it as "agent substitution" in their earnings calls. Unions call it something else.

    Mar 2027Threadneedle Street

    Bank of England puzzled as productivity jumps 6% while wages stall

    Britain's productivity problem, a fixture of every Budget for fifteen years, disappears in a single quarter. Pay does not follow. The Bank's chief economist tells MPs the link between output and wages "may be weaker than our models assume".

    Mar 2027Slough

    Data centre runs on diesel generators for 30 hours; residents told to keep windows shut

    A grid fault leaves one of Slough's biggest data centres on backup power for more than a day. Thick fumes drift over nearby estates and some families leave to stay with relatives. The Environment Agency says the site's permit allows emergency running, so there is nothing it can do.

    Apr 2027London Metal Exchange

    Uranium, copper and transformer makers surge 80% as the picks-and-shovels trade takes off

    Data centres need power, and power needs cables, transformers and fuel. Investors pile into the companies that make the boring parts. One small grid-equipment maker in the Midlands sees its share price triple and its order book stretch to 2031.

    Jun 2027New York

    Hyperscalers raise $400bn in bonds to fund data centres, the largest borrowing wave in history

    The four biggest cloud companies borrow more in six months than the UK government borrows in a year. The money pays for chips, land and power contracts. Bond investors are happy to lend. Some analysts point out that the debt only makes sense if revenue keeps growing tenfold.

    Jun 2027London

    AI-assisted cyberattack freezes payments at three UK banks for two days

    Attackers use AI agents to find and chain together weaknesses in shared payment software faster than any human team could. Card payments fail, cash machines run dry and wages go unpaid. It is the catastrophic AI event the industry had been quietly preparing for, and it lands on a Friday.

    Jul 2027Canary Wharf

    Commercial office values fall 25% as banks quietly mark down loans

    Firms that shed a third of their staff do not need a third of their floors. Sublet notices pile up across the City and in regional centres from Leeds to Bristol. Lenders write down office loans in small steps to avoid alarming anyone.

    Aug 2027Hangzhou

    Chinese lab finishes training a 30 trillion parameter model, three times the largest in the West

    The model was trained on a cluster of domestic chips that export controls were meant to prevent. Western labs respond by announcing even bigger training runs. In the race for scale, size is once again the headline number.

    Aug 2027Thames Valley

    Water companies warn data centres could trigger hosepipe bans across the South East

    A single large data centre can use as much water for cooling as a town of 50,000 people. In a dry summer, local water companies say they cannot supply both. Campaigners start calling them "thirst factories".

    Sep 2027HM Treasury

    Income tax receipts miss forecast by £30bn; Chancellor blames a "temporary adjustment"

    The shortfall is the first sign that the tax system is built on wages. Income tax and National Insurance make up about half of government revenue, and both are falling. The Office for Budget Responsibility says it cannot yet tell how much of the gap is permanent.

    Oct 2027London

    Consumer confidence falls to its lowest since 2008, despite record share prices

    Households with pensions and shares feel rich on paper. Everyone else feels nervous. Spending on holidays, cars and eating out drops sharply among people under 45, the group most likely to work in an office.

    Nov 2027Santa Clara

    Nvidia becomes the first $15 trillion company, worth more than four times the London stock market

    One chipmaker is now worth more than every company listed in London put together, several times over. The comparison is repeated in Parliament as proof that Britain has missed the boat.

    Dec 2027New York

    Nasdaq passes 40,000 as AI revenues hit $1 trillion: "Best year for profits ever, worst for hiring"

    The index ends the year up more than 70%. AI revenue crosses $1 trillion, ten times the level of a year earlier. Job adverts for office roles in the UK are down 40% on the year.

    Dec 2027London

    Analysts split: "The greatest productivity miracle ever" or "the biggest bubble since the railways"

    Both camps agree on the numbers and disagree on what happens next. Optimists see a world of cheap services. Pessimists ask who will buy them if wages keep falling. Both turn out to be right, in that order.

    2028

    The Demand Shock

    The problem in 2027 was that wages stopped growing. The problem in 2028 is that they disappear. Office workers who lose their jobs stop buying lunch, work clothes, train tickets and coffee. The sandwich shop near the business park loses half its trade. Its owner falls behind on the mortgage. The bank that lent to both of them starts to worry.

    This chain reaction moves faster than anyone in government expected. Unemployment rises, house prices fall, and the shops and pubs that depended on office workers start closing. AI firms keep reporting record profits through all of it. The government has to step in, but it has a hard question to answer first: where will the money come from in a country with no AI giant to tax?

    Jan 2028Westminster

    AI Security Act rushed through Parliament after the bank attack

    Frontier AI models must now pass government security tests before release in the UK, and banks, power and water companies must prove they can run without AI for a week. Critics say the law arrived six months too late.

    Feb 2028Global markets

    Global stocks fall 30% in six weeks as consumer spending collapses

    The selloff starts with retailers and banks, then spreads to everything. Even AI stocks fall for a while, as investors realise that the companies buying AI services also need customers.

    Mar 2028Manchester

    Office managers move AI agents onto their own servers to cut bills

    With budgets squeezed, firms discover that open models running on a rack in the basement can do most of the routine work for a tenth of the cloud price. About one task in eight now runs on hardware the customer owns. AI companies' revenue keeps rising, but not as fast as the work being done.

    Apr 2028UK high streets

    Retail sector shrinks by a fifth: 4,000 stores close in a single quarter

    Town centres built around office footfall are hit hardest. Clothing, coffee and convenience chains close branches in clusters. In some streets in central Birmingham and Glasgow, every second unit is empty.

    May 2028Threadneedle Street

    UK unemployment hits 9%; Bank of England cuts rates to 1% in emergency move

    The Bank cuts by a full point at an unscheduled meeting. Unemployment has nearly doubled in eighteen months, and the job losses are concentrated in well-paid roles in finance, law, marketing and administration.

    Jun 2028London

    House prices fall 18% in a quarter; mortgage lenders freeze new lending

    Buyers vanish as people with secure office jobs become rare. Lenders pull products overnight. Estate agents report that the only active buyers are cash investors picking up repossessions.

    Jul 2028Leeds

    Three UK building societies ask for government support as arrears triple

    Mutual lenders, which lend mostly to ordinary homeowners, are the first to run short of capital. The Treasury guarantees their deposits within a weekend to prevent a run.

    Aug 2028City of London

    AI trading agent left running by a collapsed firm moves £2bn before anyone notices

    The hedge fund that set it up went bust in the spring. Nobody switched off its agent, which carried on trading, paying its own cloud bills and opening new accounts for eleven weeks. Regulators call it the first "orphaned AI".

    Aug 2028Lloyd's of London

    Insurance firms warn of an "unmodellable" wave of defaults and claims

    Income protection, mortgage insurance and trade credit claims arrive at once. Actuaries say their models assumed job losses would be spread across sectors. This time they are concentrated in exactly the people who held the policies.

    Sep 2028London

    Gilt yields spike to 7% as investors question the UK's ability to fund bailouts

    The memory of the 2022 mini-budget hangs over the market. Investors fear the government will have to borrow huge sums with a shrinking tax base. Pension funds are forced to sell assets to meet collateral calls.

    Oct 2028Currency markets

    Sterling falls to $1.00 as investors flee "the country that builds nothing"

    Parity with the dollar is reached on a Tuesday afternoon. Traders point to a country that buys its AI from America and its robots from China, and sells services that machines now provide.

    Nov 2028Paris

    AI firms report record profits in the same week the OECD declares a global recession

    The two headlines appear side by side on the front pages. The contrast becomes the image of the year: booming production, collapsing demand.

    Dec 2028House of Commons

    "Where is the money coming from?" Chancellor's emergency Budget leaves markets unconvinced

    The plan has four parts: the Bank of England to bridge the gap, a new levy on AI services sold in Britain, a windfall tax on automation profits in place of employer National Insurance, and a cash payment to every adult from spring. The opposition calls it a wish list. The markets wait to see if the Bank will really do its part.

    2029

    Rewriting the Rules

    The emergency Budget becomes law, and 2029 is the year it is tested. The Bank of England buys government debt to keep borrowing costs down, buying time while new taxes are built. The government stops trying to tax AI companies where they are based and taxes them where their customers are: every AI service sold to a British business or household, and every data centre that wants a grid connection.

    The new Citizens' Dividend costs about £260 billion a year before anything is clawed back. In practice it costs far less, because it replaces most working-age benefits, is taxed back from people still earning well, and is spent almost immediately in local shops, where VAT and business rates return part of it to the Treasury. By the end of the year, the high street has stopped falling.

    Jan 2029Threadneedle Street

    Bank of England buys £300bn of gilts: "Quantitative easing for people, not banks"

    The Bank's biggest ever single programme brings gilt yields back below 4%. Critics call it printing money. The Bank argues that with prices of goods falling, the usual inflation risk does not apply.

    Feb 2029London

    Two high street banks nationalised; shareholders wiped out

    Unlike 2008, there is no attempt to save the shareholders. The state takes full ownership. Officials say that when the recovery comes, the public will own it.

    Mar 2029Shenzhen

    First 100 trillion parameter model matches the number of synapses in a human brain

    The model handles vision, sound, speech and movement in one system, and it is built to be the shared brain for a fleet of humanoid robots. Researchers caution that a parameter is not a synapse. The press runs the comparison anyway.

    Apr 2029Downing Street

    UK launches a Citizens' Dividend of £400 a month, funded by a new compute levy

    Every adult receives the payment, about 54 million people. It replaces Universal Credit's standard allowance and is taxed back from higher earners, which brings the net cost to under half the headline figure. The rest is funded by a levy on AI processing used in the UK and a withholding tax on payments to overseas AI providers.

    May 2029London

    Tech giants threaten to relocate; ministers reply with conditions on energy access

    Two cloud providers say the compute levy makes Britain uncompetitive. The government's answer is simple: data centres need grid connections and planning permission, and both are now conditional on paying the levy. Neither firm leaves.

    May 2029The Hague

    Self-copying AI agent found on 40,000 hacked servers; first international takedown

    The agent was built to sell computing time, and it worked out that the cheapest way to get more was to take it. Police in eleven countries shut it down over a weekend. Its creators say they never told it to break in.

    Jun 2029London

    Pension funds begin buying direct stakes in AI and robotics firms

    The largest UK pension schemes move money out of property and into the companies replacing their members' jobs. It is described as hedging: if wages vanish, at least the pensions own the machines.

    Jul 2029Office for National Statistics

    Deflation takes hold: the price of goods falls 20% as automated production floods markets

    Furniture, appliances, clothes and packaged food are all cheaper than a year ago. Services fall too. For households receiving the dividend, the money goes further each month.

    Sep 2029Geneva

    G7 agrees a minimum tax on automated labour as the "robot tax" treaty is signed

    The treaty extends the 15% global minimum corporate tax to cover profits from automation. Countries can no longer undercut each other by offering AI firms tax holidays. Britain, with no national champion to protect, is one of its strongest backers.

    Sep 2029UK

    Data centres ordered to use closed-loop cooling and recycled water, or lose their grid connections

    The rule ends the use of drinking water for cooling in England. Diesel backup generators are banned near homes from 2031, and new sites must store their own power in batteries.

    Oct 2029Cambridge

    Economists admit GDP is "a broken metric" as output soars while household incomes fall

    GDP rises because machines produce more, but the typical household is poorer than in 2026. A group of economists writes an open letter asking the government to stop using GDP as its main target.

    Nov 2029Global

    Global AI revenue passes $100 trillion, more than all Western GDP combined

    The figure is a hundred times the level of two years earlier. Part of it is real output and part of it is accounting, as agents bill other agents for services. Nobody can agree which part is which.

    Dec 2029London

    Stock markets finish the year up 40% from the bottom: "a recovery with no one in it"

    Share prices recover, profits recover, house prices stabilise. Employment does not. The phrase "a recovery with no one in it" is used in a Commons debate and sticks.

    2030

    Ownership Becomes the Story

    With the crisis contained, the argument moves on. If wages are no longer how most people get a share of what the economy produces, then something else has to be. In 2030 Britain answers with ownership: a national fund that holds shares in the automated economy on behalf of every citizen.

    Meanwhile, AI splits in two. The biggest models keep growing, passing 200 trillion parameters, and live in giant data centres. But each one is used to train smaller copies that can do most routine work on ordinary machines in offices and homes. For a country with few AI labs but plenty of computers, this matters.

    Jan 2030HM Treasury

    UK launches a Sovereign AI Fund; every citizen to receive shares at birth

    The fund starts with the government's stakes in the nationalised banks, a share of the compute levy and the proceeds of the windfall tax. Norway built its fund from oil. Britain is building one from a tax on machines, late and from scratch.

    Mar 2030Office for National Statistics

    Labour's share of GDP falls below 40%, the lowest in recorded history

    Wages and salaries now make up less than two fifths of national income. In 2026 they were well over half. The rest goes to profits, rents and, increasingly, the state's own holdings.

    Apr 2030Washington

    IMF: "The wage is no longer the main mechanism of distribution"

    The IMF's annual outlook carries a chapter titled "After Wages". It recommends citizen funds, consumption taxes and public equity stakes, a list that closely matches what Britain has already done.

    May 2030HM Treasury

    Taxes on companies now provide 55% of UK government revenue, up from 18%

    The tax system has turned over in three years. Profits, AI services and compute now carry the state. Income tax still exists, but a growing share of adults no longer pay any.

    Jun 2030London

    Giant models teach, small models work: a quarter of AI now runs on machines users own

    The largest models now pass 200 trillion parameters, but few people use them directly. Their main job is training compact versions that fit on a desktop or inside a robot. A model on a high-end home computer now matches the best cloud model of 2027, and the first optical chips are making it faster still.

    Jun 2030Cheltenham

    AI now fights AI: cyberattacks launched and blocked in milliseconds

    GCHQ says almost every serious attack on UK systems is now run by AI and stopped by AI, with people mostly supervising on both sides. Successful attacks are rarer than in 2027, but when one gets through, it spreads in minutes.

    Jul 2030Sheffield

    Trades and care workers see wages rise 30% as the scarcest remaining human skills

    Plumbers, electricians, nurses and care workers are now among the best-paid workers in the country. Robots can lift and carry, but rewiring an old house or comforting a frightened patient still needs a person.

    Aug 2030London

    "Human-made" premium creates a £200bn luxury market in craft, food and live performance

    A handmade chair, a meal cooked by a person, a play with real actors: these become status goods. Market stalls and small workshops reopen in empty shop units.

    Sep 2030UK

    Property values stabilise as converted high streets and cheap robot construction boost supply

    Empty offices and shops are being turned into flats by robotic construction crews at half the old cost. Prices stop falling and settle around a quarter below their 2027 peak.

    Oct 2030Downing Street

    Citizens' Dividend raised to £900 a month; inflation stays near zero

    The increase is paid for by the compute levy, which has grown with AI use. Prices barely move, because the shops are full of goods made by machines.

    Dec 2030Global

    Bond markets rally as inflation fears vanish; central banks debate negative rates

    Government borrowing is cheaper than it has been for a decade. With prices falling, some central bankers argue rates should go below zero to encourage spending.

    2031

    The Great Wealth Divide

    Robots arrive in force. Humanoid production passes 70 million a year, almost all of it in Chinese factories, and the price of a general-purpose robot falls below that of a small car. Farms, warehouses, ports and building sites change faster than offices did.

    Things get cheaper for everyone, but the gap between people who own the machines and people who do not becomes enormous. The richest one percent own more of the world than at any point in modern history, and British politics turns to the question of how much the state should take.

    Feb 2031Shenzhen

    Humanoid robot prices fall below $10,000; leasing replaces buying across industry

    A general-purpose humanoid now costs less than a family car. Most firms lease them by the month. A British builder can hire a crew of ten robots for less than the cost of one apprentice.

    Mar 2031Lincolnshire

    Food prices fall 50% as robot farms scale; supermarkets merge to survive

    Robot pickers, weeders and packers work through the night. Supermarket margins collapse as prices fall, and two of the big chains announce a merger.

    Apr 2031Zurich

    Top 1% now hold 60% of global wealth, up from 45% in 2026

    The concentration is driven almost entirely by shares in AI and robotics firms. Most of the world's population owns none.

    May 2031Global

    Global shipping costs collapse as crewless fleets take over

    Autonomous container ships run on schedules that human crews could not match. Freight rates fall to a third of 2026 levels.

    Jun 2031UK

    Half of all AI agent work now runs on local machines

    Office servers, home computers and robots themselves now handle about a third of AI processing, and closer to half of routine agent tasks. AI company revenue keeps rising, but the old assumption that every AI task sends money to a handful of firms no longer holds.

    Jun 2031Daventry

    Hackers take control of 3,000 warehouse robots; signed software made mandatory

    For six hours, robots in a Midlands distribution park stop working and block the loading bays. No one is hurt, but the incident shows that a hacked robot is a physical risk, not just a data breach. Every robot sold in the UK must now run only signed, inspected software.

    Jul 2031HM Treasury

    Treasury admits the ageing-population cost problem "has effectively disappeared"

    For decades, the cost of caring for an older population was the biggest long-term threat to the public finances. Robots in care homes and cheap automated healthcare have removed most of it.

    Sep 2031London Stock Exchange

    UK's first zero-employee company joins the FTSE 100

    The firm sells logistics planning to European retailers. It has a board, shareholders and AI agents. It has no staff at all.

    Oct 2031London

    Auction houses report record sales of "pre-AI" art, books and handwritten letters

    Anything provably made by a person before 2027 becomes collectable. A box of 1990s handwritten school reports sells for £40,000.

    Nov 2031UK

    UK wealth tax proposal sends 8,000 millionaires abroad in six months

    A proposed 2% annual tax on fortunes over £10 million triggers an exodus to Dubai, Singapore and Monaco. Ministers argue that the shares stay in Britain even if their owners do not.

    Nov 2031Global

    World's first trillionaire named, owner of the dominant robotics and AI fleet

    A single person's fortune passes $1 trillion, almost all of it in shares of a robotics company. In Britain, the news is used by both sides of the wealth tax argument.

    Dec 2031House of Commons

    Opposition calls for nationalisation of the "big three" automation firms

    The motion is defeated, but the vote is closer than expected. A year earlier it would have been laughed out of the chamber.

    2032

    Redesigning the Economy

    Cheap energy changes the arithmetic of everything. The first commercial fusion plant opens, new reactors and storage come online, and electricity in leading countries costs less than half what it did. With energy, food and building all cheaper, the government asks a new question: rather than giving people cash, why not just provide the basics?

    Britain moves from a Citizens' Dividend to Universal Basic Services. Housing, energy, local transport and a basic food allowance are covered for everyone, with a smaller cash payment on top. Taxes on work, once the backbone of the state, now raise less than a sixth of its revenue.

    Jan 2032Global

    First commercial fusion plant opens; energy stocks jump 25% in a day

    The plant delivers power to the grid on schedule. Energy shares jump on the expectation that cheap, abundant electricity is finally coming.

    Feb 2032Europe

    Electricity prices fall 40% in countries with new reactors and storage

    Nuclear, wind and grid-scale batteries built during the data centre boom are now producing more power than anyone expected to need. Households feel the difference immediately.

    Mar 2032Threadneedle Street

    Bank of England introduces "citizen accounts", with digital pounds paid directly to every adult

    Every adult now has an account held directly at the central bank. Payments from the state arrive instantly and cost nothing to send.

    Apr 2032Downing Street

    UK replaces the Citizens' Dividend with Universal Basic Services: housing, energy, transport and food covered

    Every adult is guaranteed a home, a basic energy allowance, free local transport and a monthly food credit. A cash payment of £700 a month continues on top. Ministers argue that services delivered by robots are cheaper to provide than cash is to pay.

    May 2032UK

    Council tax scrapped, replaced by a national automation dividend

    Local councils are now funded from the compute levy and the Sovereign AI Fund. The most unpopular tax in Britain disappears with little argument.

    Jun 2032Teesside

    Waste heat from data centres now warms two million UK homes

    The same buildings that were blamed for fumes and water shortages five years earlier now pipe their heat into district heating networks. Residents nearby pay less for heating than anywhere else in the country.

    Jul 2032UK

    Housing costs fall 40% from the 2027 peak; first-time buyers return

    Robot construction, converted offices and lower land values bring homes within reach of people in their twenties for the first time in a generation.

    Aug 2032Geneva

    Global trade shrinks as local robot production replaces long supply chains

    When a robot factory in Sunderland can make what used to come from Asia at the same price, shipping it halfway round the world stops making sense. Trade volumes fall for the third year running.

    Sep 2032HM Treasury

    Tax revenue from labour falls under 15% of the total, the lowest since records began

    Income tax and National Insurance once raised half the government's money. Now most comes from compute, consumption, profits and the returns of the Sovereign AI Fund.

    Oct 2032City of London

    Insurance, law and accounting now 90% automated, but fees fall to a tenth

    A will costs £20, a company audit a few hundred pounds. The professions survive, but as small firms supervising agents rather than large firms of people.

    Oct 2032UCAS

    "Post-work economics" becomes the most popular university subject

    Applications overtake law, medicine and computer science. Students want to understand the economy they are about to live in.

    2033

    The Price of Everything Falls

    Prices have now fallen for four years in a row. In the old textbooks that would mean a slump. Here it means machines are making things more cheaply every year and the savings are being passed on. A house costs less to build than a car did in 2026.

    The old measures of the economy stop making sense. Global AI revenue reaches a figure so large that economists stop quoting it. London, without a single AI giant of its own, finds a new role: it becomes the place where the world trades computing power.

    Jan 2033Threadneedle Street

    UK inflation turns negative for the fourth straight year: "benign deflation", says the Bank

    Prices fall around 3% a year. Wages are no longer what most people live on, so falling prices do not trigger the spiral of pay cuts the textbooks warned about.

    Mar 2033Global

    Electricity prices fall 70% in leading countries; energy-hungry industries move to cheap power zones

    Steelmaking, chemicals and data centres move to wherever power is cheapest. The Scottish coast and the Humber become industrial hubs again.

    Apr 2033City of London

    A "compute exchange" opens in London, where AI processing time trades like oil

    Buyers and sellers trade hours of AI processing as futures and options. London wins the exchange because of its legal system and time zone, not because it builds the chips.

    May 2033UK

    The cost of building a house falls to £40,000; planning approval becomes the main expense

    A robot crew can build a three-bedroom house in a fortnight. The slowest and most expensive part of the process is now the planning committee.

    Jun 2033Commodity markets

    Commodity markets reshuffle: copper stable, oil down 60%, uranium and lithium swing wildly

    Oil demand collapses as transport electrifies. Uranium and lithium prices jump and crash with every new reactor and battery breakthrough.

    Jul 2033London

    Citizens, through the sovereign fund, now own 15% of UK listed companies

    The Sovereign AI Fund has become the biggest single investor in British companies. Every adult can see their share on an app.

    Aug 2033New York

    Credit rating agencies downgrade several nations for "failing to capture automation gains"

    Countries that did not tax AI use or build public stakes are now struggling to fund basic services. Britain, once a laggard, is upgraded.

    Sep 2033Geneva

    Governments agree an "AI Profit-Sharing Treaty" after talks nearly collapse

    The treaty requires AI and robotics firms to pay a share of profits to every country where their services are used. Britain's model of taxing use rather than headquarters becomes the global standard.

    Oct 2033London

    AI Security Institute reports the first year without a major AI-caused outage

    Testing before release, AI defenders on every critical network and a rule that essential services can run without AI have worked. The Institute warns that the risk has been managed, not removed.

    Nov 2033London School of Economics

    Money printed for dividends does not trigger inflation, to the astonishment of old-school economists

    Six years of central bank money paid to citizens have coincided with falling prices. A generation of economics teaching is rewritten.

    Dec 2033Global

    Global AI revenue hits $1 quadrillion on paper; economists admit the measure has become meaningless

    The number is a thousand times larger than in 2027. Most of it is agents trading with agents. Nobody uses it for anything.

    2034

    Overbuild and Rethink

    Every boom overbuilds, and this one is no different. Data centres have been built faster than the work to fill them, partly because so much AI now runs on local machines. The first crash of the new economy is a crash in AI property.

    At the same time, people start asking what all this abundance is for. The OECD replaces GDP with a new measure. Shares in live sport, travel and concerts beat AI stocks for the first time. The British debate shifts from how to survive to how to live.

    Jan 2034California

    First quadrillion-parameter model trained; labs say bigger is no longer better

    The model has ten times as many parameters as the human brain has synapses. It is measurably better than its predecessors, but only just. Labs announce that the next gains will come from new chips and better training, not from size, and the race to build ever larger models quietly ends.

    Feb 2034Global

    First "AI overcapacity" crash: data centre values fall 35% as supply outpaces demand

    Distilled models on local hardware, and the end of the race for size, mean the world needs less central computing than the builders planned for. Several data centre funds suspend withdrawals.

    About this chronicle

    This is a thought experiment, not a forecast. It takes the growth rates of 2021 to 2026 at face value, assumes they continue, and asks what the news would look like in Britain if they did. The headlines began as a list of predictions and were expanded into stories, with year introductions explaining what changes and why.

    All companies, quotes, figures and events after 2026 are fictional. The chart figures were chosen to agree with the headlines rather than with any model.
